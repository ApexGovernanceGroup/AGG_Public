Level 1-2 Taxonomy, File Naming, and Lifecycle Starter

This public-safe starter gives leaders a practical structure for organizing knowledge, records, files, decision products, and repository content before a full client-specific taxonomy is designed.

Public-Safe Boundary

Use this as a scaffold, not as a final authority model.

This starter does not replace legal, records, security, privacy, classification, contract, or enterprise architecture requirements. A final client taxonomy should be calibrated to actual authority, systems, roles, sensitivity, retention, and retrieval behavior.

Level 1-2 Starter Taxonomy

Six top-level lanes with example Level 2 categories.

Governance and Authority

Level 2: Charters, Decision Rights, Policies, Review Rhythm

Use for the documents and evidence that define who can decide and how.

Strategy and Execution

Level 2: Objectives, COAs, Roadmaps, Measures

Use for executive intent, planning choices, funded work, and outcome tracking.

Operations and Delivery

Level 2: Programs, Projects, SOPs, Handoff

Use for operating work that must move from tasking to delivery and closeout.

Knowledge and Records

Level 2: Lessons, Critical Knowledge, Records, Archive

Use for continuity, retention, retrieval, lessons, and institutional memory.

Data, Analytics, and AI

Level 2: Data Assets, Dashboards, Models, Automation

Use for trusted data, analytical products, AI-assisted work, and automation rules.

People and Capability

Level 2: Roles, Training, Certification, Succession

Use for role expectations, proficiency, workforce evidence, and continuity risk.

File Naming Convention

Make the filename carry retrieval, status, and authority.

ORG-L1-L2-ARTIFACT-YYYYMMDD-STATUS-v##.ext

Token Purpose Example
ORG Owning organization, business unit, program, or office. AGG
L1 Level 1 taxonomy lane for the dominant body of work. GOV
L2 Level 2 category that makes retrieval and routing specific. CHARTER
ARTIFACT Plain-language product type or record family. OPERATING-RHYTHM
YYYYMMDD Date of release, decision, approval, or controlled publication. 20261007
STATUS DRAFT, REVIEW, APPROVED, ACTIVE, SUPERSEDED, or ARCHIVE. ACTIVE
v## Version number aligned to change control. v02

Example: AGG-GOV-CHARTER-OPERATING-RHYTHM-20261007-ACTIVE-v02.docx

Lifecycle Delineation

Every artifact needs a visible state.

  1. Intake

    A need, record, file, product, or evidence item enters the system.

    Control question: Who owns it and why does it exist?

  2. Classify

    Apply Level 1 and Level 2 taxonomy, owner, sensitivity, and retrieval metadata.

    Control question: Can a future user find it without knowing who created it?

  3. Draft

    Work is being built and should not be treated as approved guidance or authority.

    Control question: Is status obvious enough to prevent accidental operational use?

  4. Review

    The right owner, stakeholder, counsel, or authority reviews the content.

    Control question: What proves the right people reviewed the right version?

  5. Approve and Publish

    The approved version becomes active, discoverable, and usable.

    Control question: Where is the authoritative copy?

  6. Operate and Improve

    The artifact is used, measured, corrected, and improved through a review rhythm.

    Control question: What signal tells the owner to update it?

  7. Archive or Dispose

    The item is retained, archived, superseded, or disposed under the applicable rule.

    Control question: Which retention or continuity rule governs final handling?
